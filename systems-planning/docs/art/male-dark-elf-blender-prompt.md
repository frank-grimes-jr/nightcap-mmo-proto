# Blender production prompt · Male Dark Elf in lava-forged plate

Use the complete text below as the Blender artist's assignment. The companion [Unity prompt](male-dark-elf-unity-prompt.md) consumes its FBX and textures. All paths starting with a project folder resolve from `C:/nightcap-development-mmo/`.

## Assignment

Build one complete male Dark Elf for Nightcap in Blender 5.2. Deliver an editable, textured, skinned character with a removable closed helm and one full set of lava-forged plate. Use **Furnace Warden** for the first build. If assigned **The Last Pour** or **Ash Requiem**, substitute that suit throughout and keep the same finished body and bone hierarchy. The deliverable is the character in its bind pose. Animation clips, controllers and movement are outside this pass.

The first visual read should be a tall, lean elf carrying very heavy armor. The second should be how the armor was made. In the Warden, the shell has cooled to dark iron while the recessed grille and shutters retain heat. A pale reflection across the brow should describe the shape even with the ember color turned off. The elf should remain recognizable with the helm removed, with the visor in shadow, and when only the head and shoulders fill a small portrait.

## Build the body before fitting plate

1. Duplicate the male MPFB-derived Nightcap character into a new file. Begin at the project's recorded male height of 1.82 m barefoot, crown to sole. Record the actual measurement after the edit. Exclude the helmet, crest, hair volume and boot soles when measuring the body. Place the character centrally at ground level, facing Blender -Y, with Z up. Keep one armature centred with the body.
2. Work in the source skeleton's neutral bind pose. Preserve shoulder, elbow, hip and knee joint centers wherever possible. Create the taller-looking silhouette through a lean waist, long facial planes and fitted equipment before changing bone lengths. If a joint genuinely needs relocation, update its rest fit and every dependent bind together, and measure the result.
3. Finish the head from front, profile and underside. Give him a long angular jaw, high cheek plane, defined nose bridge, upper lids that sit over the eyes, copper-amber irises and muted indigo skin. Shape the pointed ears in thickness as well as outline. Carry the helix into the temple and keep the rear surface paintable. Build a scalp and a short swept-back ivory hairstyle that can hide independently under the helm. Design the uncovered male identity from the readable eye and ear details in the male plates.
4. Finish a continuous torso, pelvis, back, hands with distinct digits, and feet beneath the suit. Preserve the source's useful shoulder, elbow, thumb, groin and knee loops. Fit plain close underlayers for the unequipped review. Keep the underlying skin intact in the editable source. Group body coverage so a dressed export can hide covered areas while the base state restores them. Check boundaries at the jaw, armpits, wrists and ankles at close range.

## Turn the images into usable textures

1. Author separate UV space for skin and equipped plate. A workable first pass is a 2048² skin atlas and a 2048² suit atlas, with an optional separate small eye/hair sheet if the existing setup warrants it. Treat these as starting sizes and record actual dimensions. Give the visor, eyes, exposed ears and dominant shoulder enough texels to hold the portrait read. Keep unique space for asymmetric wear. Place seams under the jaw, along the helmet's rear closure, on the inner arm and under armor overlaps.
2. On a non-exporting reference plane, load the original concept PNG at native size. Mask off the background and isolate the large portrait from the small full-body view. Name the donor layers by source and surface, such as `Warden_Portrait_Grille`, `Warden_Portrait_Iron` and `Warden_Figure_Greave`. Use the **portrait's actual pixels** for the hood rim, bar edges, shoulder wear and vent interiors. Use the full-body image for the spacing and color rhythm of the tassets and greaves. Sample project skin paint for tone and paint the male features on his own UVs.
3. Align a temporary camera to each view independently. Match the outer contours with clay shading first. Stencil or project each masked crop onto only its corresponding visible faces, then bake the color into UV0 on a separate layer. For a nearly flat iron patch, clone from a clean interior patch of the photo onto the destination island. A whole screen image mapped across the cuirass will bake in background, perspective and false openings.
4. Remove broad directional cast shadows, rim lights from the studio and false silhouette edges from the projection. Preserve local color changes that belong to the material, the broad painted metal roll and heat inside the cavity. Check the result in unlit color and under a reversed key. Correct contours that appear twice, perspective-stretched links, or a highlight that visually turns a convex plate inside out.
5. Paint sides, back, underside, palms and soles into the same atlas. Build these from cropped project material samples and original strokes at the same mark size. Wrap the shoulder wear around the actual lip, continue the underlayer across the armpit, and disturb repeated patches with hand-painted transitions. Project a second side only where the source perspective supports it. Inspect seam continuity at full size and at the first few mip levels. Start with 16 px of UV-island dilation at 2048 and increase it where the mip check shows bleed.
6. Deliver sRGB base color and, for lava accents, a linear grayscale emission mask with black outside the recessed heat areas. Keep the broad orange color in the albedo so the suit reads in the project's painted shader. Keep the optional emissive intensity independently adjustable. Use a tangent-space normal map only if it materially improves a specific seam or stamp. Keep scalar data linear. Save editable paint layers or masks and flattened runtime PNGs. In the handoff, record source filename, crop or pixel bounds, destination atlas region, edits and which unseen surfaces were hand-painted. Pack or relink the resulting textures and reopen the file.

## Bind, export and inspect

- Reuse the `Male_GameRig` hierarchy. The source binding report records 53 bones. Inspect the actual hierarchy, bone rolls, rest matrices and left/right names before editing. Preserve `pelvis`, `neck_01`, `head`, and the existing clavicle, arm, hand, leg and finger chains. Use one deform skeleton for every outfit state. Retain native vertex groups. Normalize deform weights to one, limit them to four influences per vertex, and check for unweighted vertices.
- Bind skin and soft underlayers smoothly. Weight the helm to the head, collar to the upper torso, greaves to the corresponding shins, and each rigid plate primarily or wholly to the bone it follows. Keep the chain anchored consistently with its sockets. At rest, inspect the actual visor-to-eye line, chain clearance, floating plates, coplanar surfaces and contact with the floor. A bind audit and removable equipment are sufficient for this assignment.
- Keep editable source pieces grouped as body, eyes, hair, underlayer, helm, torso/shoulders, arms/hands and legs/feet. Export **one FBX containing the one armature and separately switchable render meshes**. Preserve independent helm/hair/body visibility and rest bind matrices. Follow the project's static exporter in `character-production/scripts/prepare_runtime_characters.py` for -Z forward, Y up, FBX unit scaling, deform bones, no added leaf bones and animation baking off. On an export copy, evaluate needed mesh modifiers and triangulate consistently while retaining weights and UVs. Export selected runtime objects only.
- Use a new work folder at `character-production/output/dark-elf-male/<suit>/`, with suit slug `furnace-warden`, `last-pour` or `ash-requiem`. Deliver `DarkElfMale.blend`, `DarkElfMale.fbx`, `Textures/`, a brief `handoff.md` and `Review/`. In the handoff list the active suit, units, barefoot and equipped heights, forward axis, rest pose, bone count, renderer names, which pieces to toggle in each state, material slots, maps and color spaces, and measured triangles per mesh. Label the back and any other inferred features.
- Reimport the FBX into an empty scene. Compare dimensions, boot contact, bone count, vertex groups, material slots and UV orientation against the native file. Capture front, side, rear, three-quarter, helmet detail and uncovered head. Include one clay image and one unlit atlas-color view. At 128 px figure height, the Warden should read by square shoulders and shuttered chest, Last Pour by the sloped shoulder and smooth visor, or Ash Requiem by the crest and high pale collar. At portrait scale the eye must sit behind the opening and the metal must retain a gradient through shadow. Correct visible defects before passing the files to Unity.
