# Blender production prompt · Female Dark Elf in original Nightcap robes

Use this assignment to build one static, skinned character in Blender 5.2. The [Unity assignment](female-dark-elf-unity-prompt.md) consumes the export. Paths beginning with a workspace folder resolve from `C:/nightcap-development-mmo/`.

## Character and costume

Build the adult female Dark Elf from Nightcap's [character plate](../../assets/concept-art/dark-elf/character-plate.png). Keep her lean build, angular indigo face, copper eyes, pointed ears, ivory bob and single bound braid recognizable out of armor. Deliver the complete body, original skin, hair, skeleton and **one removable robe ensemble**. Start with **Silt Reader**, a field robe for a scholar who measures old flood levels in Knellwater. Fit and paint it as an actual garment with a back, lining and closures. The robe and character should stand on their own in neutral light.

Use the established female character and Hailstone's upgraded presentation as visual references. Build all robe designs, surface marks, patterns, UVs and maps afresh. Start digital paint layers from blank fields. Study external images by eye, close them while drawing the first pattern, then compare value hierarchy and readability. Keep reference images outside the export. This assignment calls for original paint, including where the earlier male armor workflow used image projection.

## Cut and fit the garment

- **Pattern and scale.** Lay out real garment panels before sculpting folds. For Silt Reader, draw an outside wrap front, inside front, joined back panels, shaped yoke, tapered sleeves, turned cuffs, placket, sash and inset lining. Establish a hem with boot clearance on every side. Measure hem-to-floor, cuff-to-hand, collar-to-jaw and minimum braid clearance in the neutral stance. Record the values used.
- **Large shapes.** Anchor folds at the shoulder seam, belt tension, elbow bend and back pleat. Let folds open below the belt and settle into a small number of long vertical masses. Keep the hip-to-skirt transition continuous. Model the deep front overlap, underfold, cuff openings and hem turn wherever a side view sees daylight. Use a restrained thickness on exposed edges; finish the inside lip so backface culling still produces a believable opening.
- **Cloth behavior.** Use a soft broad radius on wool fold crests, tighter compressed folds in the linen underlayer and a crisp edge on ceramic or metal. Let the side slit reveal only the intended inner garment. Separate the sash from cloth where it casts a meaningful shadow. Keep boot toes visible under the hem so the character's weight and ground contact are easy to judge.
- **Topology and normals.** Follow the direction of the long folds with mesh loops. Spend geometry on the ear outline, cuff openings, collar lip and the skirt's outer edge. Paint small weave, stitch and abrasion detail. Inspect the back and underside for twisted faces, doubled surfaces, welded slit edges, visible floating cuffs or shading discontinuities. Two-sided rendering can mask a missing lining; inspect with backface culling on.

## Paint textures that another Nightcap artist can rebuild

1. Make flat-color thumbnails of the robe at 128 px figure height. For Silt Reader, choose a dark middle-value body, warmer inner strip, quieter yoke and one accent near the hand or face. Keep the face readable against the collar. Compare a grayscale thumbnail and a cool-lit thumbnail before committing to a palette.
2. Allocate separate UV0 space for skin/hair and robe materials. Start with a 2048² robe atlas as an authoring choice. Align warp direction down the hanging panels and along the sleeve. Give the outer wrap unique UV space for the flood-register marks and repair. Keep checked cloth grain at consistent physical scale across front and back. Place seams on garment seams. Dilation of 16 px at 2048 is a starting point; inspect the reduced mips for actual bleed.
3. Build paint layers in this order: flat dyed fields, restrained dye variation, broad fold color, inset lining, original border design, local wear, then stitches or fasteners. Design a small seamless wool repeat by hand and check it in a 3 × 3 grid before laying it over the atlas at low contrast. Save the source tile, its intended scale and layer opacity. The motif and damage sit on unique layers. Draw a repeat boundary next to the flat pattern so another artist can reconstruct it.
4. Check the texture on the model with a temporary unlit material. Track a register mark from the flat panel to the worn garment. Check grain at the sleeve cap, hem return and center-back pleat. At a distance, the register should merge into a border rhythm and the split lining should remain readable. If the weave flickers, reduce contrast or frequency in the paint. Sharpening the whole atlas will also sharpen the noise.
5. Export sRGB base color. Start with matte cloth driven by smooth mesh normals. Only deliver a linear normal or roughness mask if a specific woven seam or hard accessory needs it and the Unity handoff identifies a shader that consumes it. Keep the cloth non-emissive. Paint material color into folds without baking a studio cast shadow down one side of the garment. Write a short recipe listing the chosen palette, swatch values, panel dimensions or UV placement, repeat unit, layer order, brush scale, wear placement, and texture outputs. Include the editable paint source.

## Skin, export and hand off

Use one deform armature across body and outfit. Preserve the existing named hierarchy, bone rolls and rest matrices. Normalize deform weights, cap them at four influences per vertex, and check for missing weights. Weight collar/yoke mainly to upper torso, sleeve to its own arm and cuff to the forearm. A rigid clasp follows its mounting point. For a continuous skirt, anchor the waist to pelvis and grade each side into the appropriate thigh chain, retaining a stable central overlap. Inspect the weight colors in front and behind. A nearest-surface transfer can accidentally bind a left panel to the opposite thigh or pull a sleeve into the rib cage. Review these explicitly. Work in bind pose; movement, simulation, clips and controllers are deferred.

Save under `character-production/output/dark-elf-female/<robe>/`, using `silt-reader`, `kiln-oracle` or `mourning-veil`. Deliver `DarkElfFemale.blend`, `DarkElfFemale.fbx`, `Textures/`, `Review/` and `handoff.md`. Export one skeleton with separately identifiable body, hair, underlayer, robe and accessory meshes. Follow `character-production/scripts/prepare_runtime_characters.py` for deform-only FBX export, -Z forward, Y up, unit scaling, no added leaf bones and animation baking off. Keep reference meshes, boards and cameras out of the FBX. Include the source body in a restorable base state; document garment and skin section visibility.

Reopen the native file and reimport the FBX into an empty scene. Compare source and imported heights, bind pose, material slots, UV orientation and floor contact. Record bone, material, skinned-mesh and triangle counts. Capture front, profile, rear, three-quarter, uncovered head, and a close view showing cuff, lining or worked border. Include one actual unlit render, one clay render and a gameplay-size thumbnail. Identify hand-designed rear details and record which input informed each design decision. Hand this evidence to the Unity artist with the actual atlas paths and color spaces.
