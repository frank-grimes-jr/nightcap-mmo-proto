# Classes and Advancement

## Design status

Provisional, 2026-08-02. The three-class structure and respecialization principles are defined, but the class roster, names, abilities, balance values, and final name of the parallel-advancement system remain TBD.

Canonical source: [D-012: Three-class character identity and parallel advancement](../design/decisions.md#d-012-three-class-character-identity-and-parallel-advancement).

## Building a character

Every character combines three distinct classes:

1. A permanent primary class.
2. A stronger secondary subclass.
3. A more limited tertiary subclass.

The same class cannot fill more than one of these slots on a character.

Any eligible class can be a primary class on one character or a subclass on another. Final class names, themes, and ability designs will be original.

## Primary class

The player selects a primary class during character creation. This choice is permanent and supplies the class's complete ability set, making it the character's central identity.

The primary class cannot be removed during respecialization. Whether points allocated within its advancement path can be reset separately is TBD.

## Subclasses

An introductory in-world quest opens the secondary and tertiary class slots. Subclasses supply curated portions of their full class kits rather than every ability.

The secondary subclass is intentionally stronger than the tertiary subclass. Exact ability access, scaling, and restrictions for both slots are TBD.

After completing the introductory quest, the player may select any level-eligible subclass. Individual classes do not require separate unlock quests.

## Parallel advancement

Alongside normal leveling, defeating enemies and completing quests award points for a slower parallel-advancement system. Its final player-facing name is TBD.

The character earns one shared point pool that can enhance abilities associated with any of their three selected classes. Each class has its own dedicated advancement choices.

These choices let players shape the same class combination for different priorities. A solo-oriented character might invest in damage or self-sustain, while a group-oriented character might invest more deeply in a primary role such as defense.

Exact earning rates, costs, caps, prerequisites, exclusions, and balance rules are TBD.

## Equipment and subclass builds

The primary class determines which armor chassis and weapons a character may equip; selecting a subclass does not add new equipment categories. Eligible equipment can still carry statistics that support secondary or tertiary abilities, allowing hybrid builds without dissolving the primary class's equipment identity.

Specialized hybrid items, broadly useful accessories, and limited augments provide different ways to support cross-role combinations. See [Equipment and Itemization](equipment-and-itemization.md) for the provisional player-facing rules.

## Replacing subclasses

Secondary and tertiary classes can be replaced only at a designated safe location. Replacement requires currency or another meaningful gate; the exact mechanism and cost are TBD.

Once the introductory subclass quest is complete, every level-eligible subclass is available during respecialization. Players do not repeat class-specific unlock quests.

Removing a subclass also removes access to its active abilities. Every advancement point invested in that subclass returns to the character's unspent pool, so experimenting with a new combination does not destroy earned progression.
